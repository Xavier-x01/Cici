# Curator Note: Notes from Underground — Part I, Chapter 1

**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

*Notes from Underground* (1864) is the text in which Dostoevsky most directly confronts the civilizational question this chapter addresses. Part I, Chapter 1 is the right excerpt: it is the voice in its purest form, before the narrator's confessional narrative begins in Part II.

The chapter's argument is that the underground man represents the psychological consequence of grafting Western rational individualism onto a Russian soul shaped by Orthodox communal tradition. This text is the primary evidence for that claim. Without it, the exhibit is all context and no subject.

## Selection Notes

Confining the exhibit to Part I, Chapter 1 is a curatorial judgment. The full text is 130+ pages; the exhibit cannot display it. Chapter 1 contains the defining declaration and establishes the voice. For visitors who want more, the full Project Gutenberg text is available at the source URL.

The Garnett translation is used here because it is public domain. Curators should note in the exhibit that the Pevear/Volokhonsky (1994) translation is considered more accurate and should be recommended for serious engagement.

## Limits

An excerpt is not the whole text. The underground man's worldview becomes more complex across Part II. This excerpt shows the voice at its most extreme; the full text is more nuanced. Exhibit visitors must not treat the excerpt as a complete characterization.
