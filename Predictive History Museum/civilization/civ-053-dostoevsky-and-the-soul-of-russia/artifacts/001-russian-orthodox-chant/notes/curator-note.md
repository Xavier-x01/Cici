# Curator Note: Russian Orthodox Liturgical Chant (Obikkhod)

**Artifact:** civ-053-001
**Room:** entrance_artifact
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The chant is the exhibit's foundation because it represents what Dostoevsky was writing against and within simultaneously. The underground man cannot exist without this sound — his rationalism is a reaction to it, his suffering is shaped by its absence from his inner life.

Placing sound as the entrance artifact is a deliberate curatorial choice: it asks the visitor to inhabit the sensory world of nineteenth-century Russia before engaging any intellectual argument. Text-first exhibits produce readers; this ordering produces listeners who must then become readers.

## Selection Notes

The Obikkhod is the standard liturgical chant collection — the music ordinary Russians heard at parish worship, not monastic or cathedral performance. This is the right selection because the chapter concerns Russian civilization broadly, not the ecclesiastical elite.

Internet Archive holds multiple public domain recordings. The specific item referenced here should be confirmed and documented before download. Note performers, recording date, and institution before finalizing this artifact.

## Limits

Any recording is a performance by specific singers at a specific historical moment. The tradition is not the recording. The visitor should understand they are hearing one instance of an ongoing practice, not an archival document of Dostoevsky's St. Petersburg. No recording can recover the acoustic environment of an 1860s Russian parish.
