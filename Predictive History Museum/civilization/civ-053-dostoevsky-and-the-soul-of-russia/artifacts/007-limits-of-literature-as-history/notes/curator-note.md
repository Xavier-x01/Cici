# Curator Note: Limits of Literature as History

**Artifact:** civ-053-007
**Room:** caution_room
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The caution_room artifact is mandatory in every exhibit. Its presence is the museum's commitment to intellectual honesty: the exhibit will not pretend to prove what it cannot prove.

For this chapter, the specific risk is the one the chapter itself runs. Using Dostoevsky's fiction as civilizational evidence is a legitimate method — but it is a method with well-known limits that must be named explicitly.

## What the Caution Text Must Address

When the curator writes the caution essay (`original/caution-literature-as-history.md`), it should address:

1. **The constructed voice problem**: Dostoevsky's characters are not testimony. The underground man's worldview was designed by the author; it does not describe what any actual Russian person thought or felt.

2. **The selection problem**: The chapter uses specific texts. Dostoevsky wrote widely. The texts selected may not be representative of his thought as a whole.

3. **The translation problem**: English readers of Dostoevsky are reading a translator's interpretation, not Dostoevsky. Translation choices shape what seems obvious or extreme.

4. **The reception problem**: How a text was read when published may differ from how it reads now, or how it reads to a non-Russian audience.

5. **What the chapter does claim**: The caution should state clearly what the chapter's argument is and is not — it is not claiming Dostoevsky described reality. It is claiming he identified a structural tension that independent evidence confirms.
