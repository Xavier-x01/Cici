# Curator Note: Portrait of Fyodor Dostoevsky (Perov, 1872)

**Artifact:** civ-053-002
**Room:** context_room
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The Perov portrait is the most authoritative likeness of Dostoevsky and was painted with his participation. It is the image Dostoevsky himself acknowledged. Placing it in the context room — after the chant but before the texts — grounds the chapter in a specific human being rather than a literary abstraction.

The visitor who has heard the Orthodox chant is now asked to see the man who both belonged to that world and wrote against it. The ordering matters: sound first, then face, then text.

## Selection Notes

This specific portrait was completed in 1872 and now hangs in the Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow. It is available via Wikimedia Commons in high resolution from the Google Art Project scan. The painting is unambiguously public domain.

Alternative likenesses exist (photographs from the 1870s, earlier portraits) but the Perov portrait was specifically intended to capture Dostoevsky as a major Russian cultural figure. That intention is part of what the exhibit is showing.

## Limits

Portraiture is interpretation. Perov made choices about pose, lighting, and expression that shaped what we see. The gaunt, inward quality of the image is partly Perov's artistic style, not only Dostoevsky's appearance. The visitor should not read psychological states from this portrait as if it were documentary evidence.
