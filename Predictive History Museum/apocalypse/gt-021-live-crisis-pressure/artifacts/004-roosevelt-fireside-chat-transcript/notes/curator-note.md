# Curator Note: FDR Fireside Chat No. 1 — On the Banking Crisis

**Artifact:** gt-021-004
**Room:** primary_artifacts_and_texts
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The first Fireside Chat is the exhibit's primary example of institutional response to crisis pressure. It is specifically a communication act — FDR's first use of radio as a tool for direct presidential-to-public communication during a crisis. This makes it relevant to the chapter's argument about how institutions behave under pressure: one of the things institutions do is communicate.

The speech was delivered eight days after FDR's inauguration, in the middle of the banking crisis he had just inherited. The specific combination of timing and register (calm, educational, personal) makes it the defining case of the communication strategy.

## Selection Notes

This is the first Fireside Chat for a reason: it is the cleanest example of the form. Later chats covered policy, war, and politics with more complexity. Chat No. 1 is specifically about the banking crisis and specifically attempts to explain banking mechanics to a mass audience. That clarity makes it more useful as an exhibit artifact.

**Audio consideration:** FDR's delivery was as significant as the text. If a public domain audio recording is available from the FDR Library or National Archives, it should be added to `derivatives/` as a preview clip. The transcript alone loses the register that made the speech effective.

## Limits

Presidential communication is political communication. FDR's "my friends" framing was a calculated rhetorical strategy, not spontaneous warmth. The speech's explanation of banking was simplified to the point of inaccuracy in places. Visitors should read it as a document of communication strategy, not as an accurate description of how banking works.
