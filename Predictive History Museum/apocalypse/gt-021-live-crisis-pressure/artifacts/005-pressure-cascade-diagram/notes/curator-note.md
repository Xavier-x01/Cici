## Why This Artifact

Every exhibit has a `pressure_systems` room. Its purpose is to make structural forces visible as structures — not as events, but as systems. This exhibit's four preceding artifacts are all empirical: a chart, a poster, a newspaper, a speech. They are instances. The pressure cascade diagram is the pattern abstracted from those instances.

The diagram's position in the exhibit is deliberate: it follows the evidence rather than preceding it. Visitors should see the data before they see the model. The diagram then shows what the data adds up to — what kind of system was operating in 1907 and 1929–1940.

## What the Diagram Needs

The curator or designer who creates this diagram should include:

1. **Phases labeled in sequence:**
   - Trigger event
   - Initial cascade
   - Trough
   - False plateau (explicitly named and visually distinct)
   - Secondary collapse
   - Transformation / extended mobilization / resolution

2. **Directional arrows** showing the flow of pressure through phases

3. **Asymmetry marked**: the drop phase is typically faster than the recovery phase

4. **A model disclaimer** built into the diagram: "This is a model, not a prediction"

5. **Reference to the empirical cases**: the diagram could annotate specific years from the Depression as markers on the model curve

## Format

SVG vector format, sized for exhibit display. The diagram should be readable at poster size and at screen size. Avoid dense text; label phases with short terms and put explanation in the wall label / curator note.
