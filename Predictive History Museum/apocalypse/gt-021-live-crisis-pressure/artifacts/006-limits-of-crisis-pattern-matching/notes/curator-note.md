# Curator Note: Limits of Crisis Pattern-Matching

**Artifact:** gt-021-006
**Room:** caution_room
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The caution_room artifact is mandatory in every exhibit. For the Apocalypse book's chapters, the caution takes a specific form: the book's core method is identifying crisis pressure patterns across history. The risk of that method is the one this artifact addresses — using pattern recognition to generate false certainty about what will happen next.

The exhibit has shown the visitor a pressure cascade shape and told them it recurred across two historical cases separated by two decades. The instinct is to ask: "So if I see this shape again, I can predict how it will end?" The caution artifact answers that instinct directly.

## What the Caution Text Must Address

When the curator writes `original/caution-crisis-pattern-matching.md`, it should address:

1. **Pattern families are not laws**: historical crises share family resemblances, not identical structures. The differences between cases are as significant as the similarities.

2. **Sample size**: two cases (1907, 1929–1940) are not a statistical basis for generalization. The chapter presents them as illuminating examples, not as a sufficient data set.

3. **Unknown variables**: every crisis occurs in a specific institutional, political, and technological context. Matching the pressure shape does not match those contexts.

4. **The retrospective fallacy**: crises look patterned in retrospect because we select the cases that fit the pattern. Crises that did not follow the signature may simply be underrepresented in the analysis.

5. **What the chapter does claim**: the caution should state clearly what the chapter is and is not claiming. It claims that understanding pressure cascade signatures is useful for recognizing dynamics as they develop — not for predicting outcomes.
