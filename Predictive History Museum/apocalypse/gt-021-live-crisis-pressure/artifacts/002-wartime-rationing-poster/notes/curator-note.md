## Why This Artifact

The rationing poster marks the temporal extension of the crisis arc. Most visitors understand the Depression as ending; this artifact shows that the pressure continued in a different form through the early 1940s. The chapter's argument about crisis pressure includes the observation that pressure does not simply end — it converts.

The contrast between the confident institutional tone of the 1943 poster and the panic evident in the 1907 newspaper headline is itself an argument: by 1943, the American state had developed the administrative capacity to manage scarcity rather than simply react to it.

## Limits

Government posters communicate state intention, not civilian experience. Compliance with rationing was uneven; black markets existed. The poster's confident public-facing design does not establish that the underlying pressure was smoothly managed. The wall label should distinguish between institutional messaging and lived experience.
