# Curator Note: New York Times Front Page, October 24, 1907

**Artifact:** gt-021-003
**Room:** primary_artifacts_and_texts
**Date of note:** 2026-05-18

## Why This Artifact

The 1907 Panic is the chapter's secondary case alongside the Depression. It shows the same pressure-cascade signature — bank runs, institutional failure, emergency coordination — at a moment before the regulatory and monetary infrastructure that would respond to 1929 existed. The comparison establishes that the pressure pattern predates its institutional response.

The newspaper front page is specifically valuable because it captures the moment of live crisis pressure — what the crisis looked like to a reader on the day it was happening. This is distinct from retrospective analysis. The chapter argues that living inside a crisis pressure event is structurally different from analyzing it in retrospect; the newspaper page is evidence of the lived experience.

## Rights and Access

NYT TimesMachine requires a subscription. This artifact is currently classified `external_link_only`.

**Alternative to investigate:** The Library of Congress Chronicling America collection (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov) holds public domain newspapers from 1907, including coverage of the Panic from non-NYT publications. A contemporaneous newspaper from a public domain source would allow local storage and would not require subscription access.

Recommended search: Chronicling America, October–November 1907, keywords "panic," "trust," "Knickerbocker."

## Limits

A single newspaper front page is one publisher's framing of one day. The Panic of 1907 ran from October through November; a single date captures one moment of a multi-week event. Visitors should not treat this headline as a complete account of the panic.
